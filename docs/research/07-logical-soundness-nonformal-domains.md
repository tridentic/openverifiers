# Cross-domain logical-soundness verification for non-formal domains — research brief

Status: **Research brief [open]** — feeds future-thesis section of the OpenVerifiers paper. Not a claim of a working system, current or planned. Companion briefs: `01-frames-kr.md`, `02-rdf-owl-reification.md`, `03-dl-abox-tbox.md`, `04-path-analysis-sem.md`, `05-bayesian-networks.md`, `06-property-graphs.md`. Literature survey (§2) populated from web research; every other claim source-tagged `[sourced]` or `[hypothesis]`.

## 0. What it is

OpenVerifiers verifies claims in domains with deterministic ground truth (math, physics derivation, code): Fact nodes, Transformation rules with preconditions, sympy/z3 hooks, DAG traces, a benchmark gate on auto-extracted grammars. This brief asks the extension question: is there a domain-general notion of *logical soundness* that is checkable even where **no deterministic ground truth exists** — covering both decision-making (was the conclusion supported by the stated premises?) and action-taking (did the action follow from the decision, and satisfy stated constraints?) — instantiable per domain through the existing plugin interface, the way new grammars register without core-engine changes?

Scope guardrails, restated from the brief: no 100% formal proof is aimed at — the target is a *higher-level structural check* (premises present, steps traceable, contradictions detectable), not provable truth. Domains are not assumed equally formalizable; classifying them is part of the deliverable. No product/UI/business-model design; this is engine/theory research only.

## 1. Domain-by-domain findings (tags: (a) formally verifiable today, (b) partially formalizable, (c) structurally checkable only)

For each domain: what counts as a *premise*, an *inference step*, and an *action*; 2-3 concrete examples; classification with rationale. Premise-truth (whether a stated premise is *true* in the world) is never checkable by the engine in any domain — the engine checks that premises are declared, typed, and consistently used. That is stated once here and assumed in every row.

Reading of the classification: (a) domains give the engine a *derivation check* (entailment). (b) domains give a *mixed check*: the mechanical layer is checked as (a); the judgment layer degrades to constraint/consistency. (c) domains give only *compliance + consistency*: the action is checked against declared policy and the stated reasons must be internally consistent and traceable to premises — soundness in the proof sense is undefined here. This smooth degradation is the load-bearing observation of the whole brief; §3 argues the schema stays constant across all three levels.

## 2. Literature survey (populated from web research — see survey files `/tmp/survey-*.md`)

*Survey iv — medicine/business/economics (completed; sources at end of §2).*

**Medicine: rule formalization exists and is the direct precedent for a medical grammar.** MYCIN (Shortliffe, 1970s) encoded infectious-disease expertise as ~500-600 backward-chaining IF-THEN rules; Arden Syntax standardized clinical knowledge as discrete *Medical Logic Modules* (ANSI/HL7), event-triggered, each MLM enough knowledge for one decision, trivalent logic (true/false/null — null = incompleteness, the same role as OpenVerifiers' first-class Unknown). MLMs are explicitly modular and expert-auditable; adoption was limited by the "curly-braces problem" (unstandardized local data access), syntax complexity, and missing compiler — an engineering lesson for any hosted grammar authoring surface. Bayesian networks formalize the *probabilistic* alternative primitive for diagnosis (Heckerman; scoping review reporting many AUCs > 0.75; diagnostic-assistant vignette accuracy 93%). Lesson for the brief: medicine already supplies both a structural-rule precedent (Arden/MLM ≈ grammar + event trigger) and evidence that the truth-level primitive is probabilistic, not deterministic — supporting classification (b) `[sourced — survey iv]`.

**Business: decision *process* audit, not decision correctness.** COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (1992, rev. 2013): 5 components, 17 principles assessed as present/functioning, ~82% practitioner adoption; principles-based, therefore "formal enough for audit, reliant on managerial judgment" — the honest ceiling of business formalization, and it matches classification (c). Spetzler's Decision Quality is the closer precedent: *six requirements for a high-quality decision* — appropriate frame, creative alternatives, relevant information, clear values/trade-offs, sound reasoning, commitment to action — explicitly normative, auditable by checking whether the requirements were met. This is exactly the "decide against declared criteria" shape §1 assumed; DQ is now the named prior art. No standardized primary "decision audit" methodology comparable to COSO exists — an evidence gap, not a settled field `[sourced — survey iv]`.

**Economics: the only (c)-adjacent domain with a formal consistency standard.** Revealed preference (Samuelson 1938): WARP — if A chosen over B while both affordable, B must not be chosen over A in comparable conditions — an *empirically testable* consistency condition; observed violation (endowment effect) is documented. Savage's SEU axiomatizes choice under uncertainty (completeness, transitivity, sure-thing principle). Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky 1979) documents systematic deviation — i.e., economics has both the consistency axioms and a literature on their observed violation. For the brief: WARP-style checks are the paradigm of *non-derivational verification* — a soundness standard that is formal, mechanical, and checks consistency of choices without any ground truth about the "right" choice `[sourced — survey iv]`.

*Survey iii — agent action verification (completed).*

**Action verification prior art clusters into five paradigms, none of which checks decision *content* against user-agreed rules.** (1) *Alignment-first*: Constitutional AI embeds principle-driven self-evaluation into model reasoning (high compliance in controlled evals) — but the check is internal to the checked system, which is precisely the circularity this brief's external-grammar design avoids. (2) *Adversarial/cooperative verification*: debate and judge agents can surface failures but empirical work shows single persuasive adversaries subvert debate outcomes; guarantees are idealized (PSPACE-level only for computationally unbounded debaters). (3) *Reward-based*: recursive reward modeling (Irving, Christiano, Amodei 2018) scales oversight; the reward-hacking / specification-gaming literature (Amodei et al. "Concrete Problems in AI Safety"; Krakovna et al.'s specification-gaming catalogue) documents the loophole shape — literal objective met, intent violated — confirming §5's loophole definition has named prior art. (4) *Formal orchestration model checking*: LTL/temporal-logic specifications over an orchestration model with the LLM as a *non-deterministic oracle* (AgentVerify-style tooling); guarantees are compositional but conditional on the environment model — they verify the *loop* (memory integrity, tool-call protocol, human-in-the-loop boundaries), not the *reasoning content* of any step. (5) *Runtime enforcement*: schema validation (JSON Schema), ACL/authorization, step budgets, tool-call lifecycles, and argument-level provenance (entities/activities/agents); untrusted tool arguments are a documented primary unsafe-behavior source; benchmarks (ToolRM, SafeToolBench, AgentSafetyBench) evaluate tool-use correctness/safety `[sourced — survey iii]`.

**Where the brief's grammar approach sits in this map:** (4)+(5) are the closest mechanical relatives — guard-style checks on structured artifacts with provenance — but both stop at *protocol and shape* (was the call well-formed, authorized, budgeted, recorded). None verifies that the *decision the agent acted on* is supported by the premises it declared, under rules the user agreed to. That is the unoccupied cell: domain-grammar verification of decision content + action-guard semantics, external to the agent, with a replayable certificate. The survey's recommendation of layered assurance (runtime schema + provenance + formal orchestration invariants + sentinels) is compatible — the grammar layer slots in as the *decision-content* layer those paradigms assume but do not provide `[hypothesis, position]`.

*Survey i — formal argumentation + legal reasoning formalization (completed).*

**Argumentation theory is the mature, named prior art for non-derivational soundness; the brief inherits its vocabulary wholesale.** Dung's abstract argumentation frameworks (arguments + binary attack relation; acceptability semantics) are the semantic foundation; structured frameworks add content: ASPIC+ distinguishes strict vs defeasible rules and builds argument inference trees with undercutting and preference-based defeat; DeLP/t-DeLP does defeasible logic programming with warrant by dialectical analysis; Pollock's OSCAR (defeasible justification) and Nute's defeasible logic (strict/defeasible rules + undercutting defeaters, efficient fragments) complete the foundations. Toulmin's claim–data–warrant–backing–rebuttal–qualifier is the informal ancestor of the schema §3 proposes. Walton's compendium of argumentation schemes (expert opinion, analogy, cause–effect, …) supplies the scheme + critical-question machinery §3/§5 uses. Carneades formalizes proof standards (scintilla, preponderance, clear and convincing, beyond reasonable doubt) and burden of production/persuasion — the first-class treatment of "how strong must the support be," directly usable as a per-domain grammar parameter. Case-based legal reasoning (HYPO/CABARET/CATO: factors, dimensions, analogical retrieval; value-based argumentation in Bench-Capon) is the computational precedent for analogical inference — a non-derivational inference kind that still supports mechanical factor checks. The Argument Interchange Format (AIF; with AIFdb, ARG Tech) is an RDF/RDFS domain-neutral ontology for argument graphs; LKIF is the legal-ontology counterpart, and Carneades exports to both — evidence that the "one schema, many domains" claim is *built and shared tooling*, not theory-only. Sergot et al.'s formalization of the British Nationality Act is the canonical statute-as-logic-program encoding, with temporal/predicate conditions — a direct template for a law grammar's mechanical layer. Evidence gaps: no standardized reproducible statute → argument-graph → AIF/LKIF pipeline; LLM integration with argumentation frameworks is immature `[sourced — survey i]`.

**Why the brief's engine is positioned to absorb this line of work rather than compete with it:** argumentation formalisms evaluate *given* arguments (who wins under which semantics/burden); they do not supply the fact-type/context-typing layer (units, dimensions, declared attributes) or the solver-backed computational layer (sympy/z3) that OpenVerifiers provides, and they have no action axis. The mapping is one-directional and clean: a domain grammar declares schemes as transformations, critical questions as preconditions, proof standard as a declared verdict threshold `[hypothesis, position]`.

*Survey ii — LLM consistency/fact-checking/stepwise verification (completed).*

**LLM-reasoning verification is the immediately adjacent literature and splits into five families, only one of which is deterministic; the brief's grammar approach is the deterministic family's generalized form.** (a) *Inference-time sampling/aggregation*: self-consistency (Wang et al.) samples N CoT traces, majority-votes — consensus as a *proxy* for correctness, no per-step check. (b) *Process reward models*: PRM800K + "Let's Verify Step by Step" (Lightman et al.) train step-level verifiers with human labels — a *learned, approximate* step oracle, annotation-expensive, benchmark-bounded; the learned-oracle alternative to declared-rule checking. (c) *NLI/entailment contradiction detection*: the survey found classical NLI applied to CoT inconsistency is *scarce in the literature*; FEVER and FactCC remain outcome-level benchmarks — a genuine evidence gap, which confirms §4: text-in, proof-strength verification has no settled foundation; it is approximate and benchmark-constrained. (d) *Neuro-symbolic formalization* — the family the brief sits in: MATH-VF translates natural CoT into a formal language and checks statements with external solvers (SymPy/Z3), producing corrective feedback; VeriCoT extends this beyond math by extracting core arguments, formalizing steps into first-order logic, and producing solver-based proofs with human-readable audits, explicitly targeting step grounding and *exact premise identification*. VeriCoT is the closest prior art to §4's extraction-middle-path proposal, with the documented cost: coverage depends on the formalizer, and formalization errors are invisible to the mechanical check. (e) *Self-refinement*: CoVe, debate variants, SAVeR — iterative self-correction, empirically fragile (documented "Degeneration of Thought"); single-agent self-review is not a reliable verifier without external evidence/tooling `[sourced — survey ii]`.

**Position against this map:** (b)'s learned verifiers and (d)'s formalizers both check *steps*, but (b) is statistical and non-reproducible (different samples → different judgment; no certificate the user can replay), while (d) keeps the free-text→formal step inside the pipeline it verifies. The grammar approach differs on both axes: rules are *declared by the user* (agreed ground truths, diffable, auditable) and the verdict is *deterministic and replayable*; the formalization step is either self-asserted by the agent (structured claim stream) or performed upstream of verification, never inside it. These are the two properties the LLM-checking literature does not provide, and they are exactly the properties a certificate-based verification product needs `[hypothesis, position]`.

**Literature survey — evidence gaps (all four surveys):** no standardized statute→argument-graph→AIF/LKIF pipeline; immature argumentation-framework integration with LLMs; classical-NLI-→-CoT inconsistent-detection work scarce; no strong primary standardized "decision audit" methodology comparable to COSO; no head-to-head evaluations jointly measuring formal verification, runtime monitoring, and debate on the same agent population; no standardized provenance interop profiles for agent runtimes `[sourced — surveys i-iv]`.

## 3. Can one abstract schema represent all these domains? (task 4)

**Position: yes — a single structural schema (premise / inference-step / claim / action / constraint) covers all domains, with only the rules that fill it domain-specific. The evidence is that this exact bet was already made and validated in prior work, most consequentially the Argument Interchange Format (AIF).** Details:

- **AIF is the existence proof for argument structure, not semantics.** AIF was designed as a domain-neutral interchange format for argument structure (nodes: information / rule-of-inference / preference; edges: support / attack / conflict) and has been applied across law, medicine, and e-commerce argumentation `[sourced — survey i]`. It is precisely the "one schema, many domains" claim, restricted to arguments; OpenVerifiers' fact/transformation graph is the same bet for derivations.
- **Argumentation schemes (Walton, Reed, Macagno) are the rule-filling layer.** A scheme is a generic inference template (argument from expert opinion, from analogy, from rule) with named premises and *critical questions*; a domain instantiates schemes, and checking = verify premise presence + answer the critical questions. Same schema, domain-specific scheme library `[sourced — survey i]`. This maps 1:1 onto OpenVerifiers: scheme = transformation family, premise = input facts, critical questions = additional precondition predicates.
- **What the schema must add beyond AIF/argumentation work: the action. AIF is about arguments, not acts.** Existing argumentation formalisms stop at claims. Action-taking needs a second axis: (pre-state facts) + action-node + (post-state facts), with a *guard* (may the action fire given pre-state and constraints?) and an *effect predicate* (does post-state satisfy declared consequences?). This is not a schema change to OpenVerifiers: an action is representable as a transformation whose inputs are pre-state facts, whose output is post-state facts, and whose precondition is the guard. Zero new node kinds — the fact/transformation shell absorbs actions `[hypothesis, but directly entailed by the repo's own §1.2 design where a transformation maps input facts to an output fact]`.
- **Why the schema stays constant across (a)/(b)/(c).** The three levels differ only in what the *filling rules* are allowed to assert: (a) entailment relations (computable via sympy/z3), (b) entailment for the mechanical layer + scheme-with-critical-questions for the judgment layer, (c) pure constraint/consistency predicates. Because the verifier checks "does this step instance satisfy the declared rule for its type" — never "is the rule's semantic claim true in the world" — the check procedure is identical; only the rule library's semantics degrade. Evidence for feasibility at each level comes from the surveys; the *structural uniformity* claim is `[hypothesis]`, argued, not proven.
- **Honest negative:** in (c) domains the word "soundness" keeps only a compliance meaning: *the stated reasons are internally consistent, traceable, and the action satisfies declared constraints*. It cannot mean "the reasoning was correct," because correctness is undefined without ground truth. Any document (paper, thesis) must state this explicitly; claiming more is where the approach would become unsound.

## 4. Free-text vs structured-DSL input (task 5) — the hard open question

**Position: free-text reasoning cannot be verified at proof strength directly; it can be verified only (i) approximately, at the level of local entailment/consistency, or (ii) exactly, after structure is extracted — and then the extraction step, not the verification, is the weak link. A hybrid is the evidentially strongest framing: free text as source, a typed structure as the verified artifact, extraction itself scored by consistency metrics. No build recommendation; this section states tradeoffs.**

- **What free-text gives up.** Direct text-in → verdict-out checking exists only at natural-language-inference (NLI) strength: claim pairs classified as entail/contradict/neutral. This is approximate, benchmark-bounded, and cannot certify *why* a step is valid — it produces a probability-like score, not a derivation `[sourced — survey ii]`. FEVER-style pipelines (document retrieval → claim verification) and fact-consistency measures live in this regime `[sourced — survey ii]`. For a verifier whose product is an auditable trace, NLI verdicts are evidence, not certificates.
- **What structure gains.** With a declared claim stream (what the agent *claims* it did: "step n applies rule R to facts {a,b}") the engine checks mechanically and exactly, emits a certificate, and can reject with a reason — the physics `candidate_proof` in the repo already demonstrates this shape. The cost: the input burden shifts to the agent (it must emit structured claims), and any structure the agent emits that *misrepresents* its actual text/reasoning passes verification silently — garbage-in risk moves from the check to the extraction/assertion.
- **The extraction middle path.** LLM parses free text into schema instances; engine verifies mechanically. This inherits exactness for the structure and inherits extraction error as the dominant failure mode: a wrong parse is *not* visible to the mechanical checker. Mitigations exist (NLI-consistency between text spans and extracted claims; requiring the agent to emit claims itself rather than having a second model extract them — self-asserted structure at least bounds the representation gap) `[hypothesis]`.
- **What is genuinely open.** (i) Whether self-asserted structure suffices to make the representation gap audit-proof, or whether independent extraction + cross-check is required; (ii) how to score extraction reliability so the system can report "verification confidence" honestly; (iii) whether any free-text-in mechanism can ever yield a *deterministic, reproducible* verdict, which is the property that makes OpenVerifiers' traces reusable as evidence. These are stated as open questions, not settled.

## 5. What "detecting a fallacy" / "detecting a loophole" means formally in non-formal domains (task 6)

**Fallacy — working definition.** In an (b)/(c) domain, a *fallacy* is an inference step that instantiates an argumentation scheme but fails a critical question of that scheme, or whose stated premises fail the domain's declared support relation for the conclusion. Checkable form: (1) scheme matching — the step is tagged with a scheme from the domain grammar; (2) critical-question audit — each question is a declared predicate over the facts, evaluated mechanically; (3) support check — premises→conclusion relation verified against the declared rule. Example in business: "expert opinion → buy" instantiates the *expert-opinion scheme*; critical question "is the source actually expert in this exact domain, and cited?" is a predicate; failure = fallacy detected as *undeclared/unsupported premise*, which is exactly what the engine can flag without knowing the true answer `[hypothesis, anchored in scheme+critical-questions prior art — survey i]`.

**Loophole — working definition.** A *loophole* is an action or derivation that satisfies every literal constraint in the grammar while violating the intended semantics. Detection is possible only by making intent explicit or by an external oracle; there is no internal, purely mechanical loophole detector. Two concrete mechanisms:
1. **Anti-goal clauses.** Grammar declares prohibited end-states ("no action may result in state S") in addition to permitted mechanics. This is the formalization of specification gaming: the reward-hacking literature documents cases where literal objectives were met and intent violated, which is exactly the loophole shape `[sourced — survey iii]`.
2. **Oracle-gated monotonic tightening** (the mechanism OpenVerifiers already uses for grammar extraction). A human or test suite flags a validated trace as "technically compliant, wrong in intent"; the flagged case becomes a new *must-reject* benchmark item; the grammar is tightened until the case fails. Loophole detection thereby becomes *an iterative process with human participation*, not a check — honest and bounded `[sourced — the repo ARCHITECTURE.md §5.2 benchmark gate; generalized here to rule updates, hypothesis]`.

Both definitions bottom out in the same principle: the engine detects *deviation from declared structure*; the user supplies declared intent. Detecting that literal-compliant actions violate unstated intent is, in general, undecidable-ish (it requires knowing the author's intent) — the brief's honest answer is that loophole *resistance* is achieved by declaration + tightening, never by inspection alone.

## 6. Spec outline — grammar + verifier for the abstract schema, without core-engine rewrites

**What the grammar defines.** Given the invariant schema (premise / inference-step / claim / action / constraint), a domain grammar declares: (1) *context schema* — typed Fact kinds (entities, attributes with types/dimensions, allowed statuses), the user-agreed ground truths of the case; (2) *step vocabulary* — which node kinds the domain uses and which edges are legal (a (c) domain may declare only premise→claim→action; an (a) domain declares full derivation edges); (3) *rule/scheme library* — transformations (entailment relations via sympy/z3) and/or argumentation schemes with critical questions as predicates, per domain; (4) *action rules* — for each action kind: guard (precondition over pre-state facts + constraints) and effect predicate (post-state facts); (5) *declared semantics level* (a/b/c) — which the verifier writes into the certificate so a reader knows what "passed" means; (6) *anti-goal clauses* — prohibited end-states, for loophole resistance.

**What the verifier checks.** (1) load-time static lint — grammar well-formedness: every fact kind referenced exists, every rule precondition references declared attributes, target types match rule outputs ("static typing at author time"); (2) per-step checks — inference steps: precondition + relation + output typing, existing engine path; action steps: guard on pre-state, effect predicate on post-state, both mechanical; (3) global consistency — fact id-dedup contradiction detection (same id, conflicting value) and derived-vs-truth conflict, existing DAG invariants; (4) traceability — every claim reachable from declared premises through valid steps, acyclic (existing engine guarantees); (5) scheme audit — critical questions evaluated, unanswered critical questions render the step *conditionally passed* (flagged, not failed), matching how (b) domains must behave; (6) certificate — per-claim pass/fail/conditional with the rule chain and the semantics level, rendered from the existing trace store.

**What it explicitly cannot check.** Premise truth in the world (all domains); judgment correctness in (c) domains; model-selection validity in (b) STEM domains (only stated applicability conditions); intent vs literal compliance beyond declared anti-goal clauses; probabilistic or statistical correctness; anything the agent failed to declare (unstated assumptions are invisible unless the grammar forces declaration); grammar quality itself (wrong rules are the existing benchmark-gate problem, not solved here).

**Plugin interface.** No core rewrite required: grammars already register fact schemas + transformations + solver hooks without touching the engine (§5 of ARCHITECTURE.md). The action axis needs no new node kind — actions are transformations over pre/post fact states with precondition-as-guard (argued in §3). The only *new* surface area is (i) a grammar field declaring the semantics level and (ii) the action effect-predicate hook kind — both grammar/hook additions within the existing plugin boundary. The benchmark gate (§5.2) extends from grammar extraction to rule updates by treating each tightening as a new must-reject case; that is a policy change, not an engine change.

## 7. What is NOT solved by this research

- **Premise truth, in every domain.** Garbage in, verified garbage out; premises are user-declared `truth`.
- **Judgment correctness in (c) domains** — business calls, service resolutions, legal outcomes, diagnoses. Compliance and consistency are checkable; quality is not.
- **Free-text verification at proof strength** (§4). Directly checking prose is NLI-approximate and produces no certificate; structure extraction reintroduces an error layer.
- **Loophole detection in general.** Only declared anti-goals + oracle-gated tightening; unstated intent is undecidable-ish and remains human-bound.
- **Model-selection validity in STEM word problems.** The engine verifies the derivation *given* the model; choosing the right abstraction is checkable only at stated-applicability level.
- **Auto-extraction of grammars.** Existing known-open; the benchmark gate is necessary, not sufficient.
- **Universal root logic** (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.2) — deliberately out of scope here as there.
- **Probabilistic correctness and statistical claims** (biology, medicine, economics predictions). No ground truth to check against; the axiom-consistency check in economics is normative, not predictive.
- **External world effects.** Verifying the *policy* that produced an action is not verifying the *outcome* the action caused in the world.
- **Anything the agent omits declaring.** The verifier sees only the claim stream; unstated assumptions are invisible absent forced declaration.

*End of brief.*
