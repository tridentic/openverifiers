# English Grammar - OpenVerifiers

## Supported domains

### 1. Foundations of Grammar

- **Parts of Speech (Advanced)**
  - Nouns (types, countable/uncountable, collective nouns, abstract nouns)
  - Pronouns (personal, possessive, reflexive, demonstrative, indefinite, relative, interrogative)
  - Pronoun-antecedent agreement
  - Adjectives (types, order of adjectives, comparison)
  - Adverbs (types, position, degree)
  - Verbs (transitive, intransitive, linking, auxiliary, modal)
  - Prepositions (simple, compound, phrase prepositions)
  - Conjunctions (coordinating, subordinating, correlative)
  - Interjections

- **Determiners & Articles**
  - Definite and indefinite articles (a, an, the)
  - Zero article usage
  - Demonstratives (this, that, these, those)
  - Possessives (my, your, his, her, its, our, their)
  - Quantifiers (some, any, much, many, few, little, several, enough)
  - Numbers and numerals

### 2. Verbs & Verb Phrases

- **Tenses (All 12 Forms)**
  - Present (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous)
  - Past (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous)
  - Future (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous)
  - Sequence of tenses
  - Time expressions with tenses

- **Right Form of Verbs**
  - Subject-verb agreement (complex cases)
  - Verb patterns (infinitive vs. gerund)
  - Verbs followed by specific prepositions
  - Problem verbs (lie/lay, sit/set, rise/raise)

- **Auxiliary & Modal Verbs**
  - Primary auxiliaries (be, do, have)
  - Modal auxiliaries (can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to)
  - Semi-modals (need, dare, used to)
  - Modal perfects (must have, should have, could have)
  - Modal verbs for probability, obligation, permission, advice

- **Non-Finite Verbs**
  - Gerunds (forms, functions, gerund vs. infinitive)
  - Infinitives (bare infinitive, to-infinitive, split infinitive)
  - Participles (present, past, perfect)
  - Participle phrases

- **Causative Verbs**
  - Make, have, get, let, help
  - Causative structures (active and passive)

- **Subjunctive Mood**
  - Present subjunctive
  - Past subjunctive
  - Subjunctive in conditional sentences
  - Subjunctive after specific verbs and expressions
